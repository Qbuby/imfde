Making Devin the first line of response

Reducto was running Devin on on-call before Devin Oncall existed, using Devin automations and a homegrown Slack bot that pulled Devin into each new incident channel.

"Devin has access to all of our engineering context and our codebase. So it's the natural product to stick in when it comes to investigating incidents and handling alerts."

— Raunak Chowdhuri, Co-founder and CTO, Reducto

With Devin Oncall, Reducto configured Devin as the first responder to most engineering alerts. A small set of critical alerts, where production is impacted, still page an engineer directly. Everything else goes to Devin first.

The team connected Devin to incident.io, its on-call system, and gave it the ability to page anyone on the team. Devin's rules tell it to bring in a human when an issue is serious or needs someone in the loop. For medium-severity issues, Devin often opens a pull request, tests it, and only then pages an engineer to review, merge, and deploy. The engineer can handle it from their phone instead of opening a laptop.

Engineers used to get paged three to five times a week on call. Now, for the most part, they don't get paged at all.

"For the most part, we don't have engineers get paged anymore. Engineers no longer complain about being on call. I don't hear that in the office anymore."

— Raunak Chowdhuri, Co-founder and CTO, Reducto

Catching an incident before the alerts did

In one of the first weeks with Devin Oncall, Reducto pointed it at its lower-priority alerts. Devin spotted a concerning trend across several alerts that had fired the same day, even though no high-priority alert had gone off, and opened an incident in incident.io on its own, with context on what it thought the problem was.

Reducto's production alerts fired later, as failed requests started climbing. By then the team already knew which part of the stack to investigate. Devin had bought them 10 to 15 minutes, which reduced the impact of the incident dramatically.

An agent on the on-call team

During live incidents, Devin now investigates alongside the team. It follows the conversation in the incident channel and jumps in with findings, instead of waiting in a single thread to be asked. Between incidents, Devin sends recaps of the issues it found over the past week, the patterns and trends behind them, and the pull requests its triage opened to fix small production issues.

After the first week or two, Raunak stopped sending feedback on the product. Not because the team stopped using it, but because it was working.

"It really feels like Devin is a member of our team. It's always on call with us."

— Raunak Chowdhuri, Co-founder and CTO, Reducto
