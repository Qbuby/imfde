FDE System Design: Designing AI Systems Under Customer Constraints

Work through an FDE system-design case with an explicit processing boundary, document permissions and a two-second answer budget. Distinguish private connectivity from approved processing, and calculate what the latency target permits.

BY LUKAS HOFFMANN · FDEINTERVIEWS EDITORIAL · UPDATED NOVEMBER 10, 2026 · 9 MIN READ

FDE system design starts by turning a customer's requirements into a system you can test and operate. Establish the permitted processing boundary, document permissions, workload and acceptance criteria before selecting a model or drawing the request path. Then show what breaks when a requirement changes. This worked RAG case exposes two easy mistakes: treating private connectivity as permission to use an external processor, and treating a fast first token as a complete answer. The numbers are fictional design inputs, not a customer benchmark or a claim about a particular company's interview.

What makes it different from the textbook round

Scaling, correctness and reliability still matter. Customer-facing deployment adds constraints whose owners may sit outside the engineering team: security, data owners, procurement and the people operating the system. A few thousand registered users does not define peak demand; ask about simultaneous activity, prompt lengths, bursts and downstream limits.

Make each constraint observable:

RequirementClarifying questionEvidence to retain
Processing boundaryWhich systems may receive prompts and documents?Approved data-flow map
PermissionsWhich source policy controls each document?Allowed and denied request cases
LatencyFirst content or complete answer, at what load?Request-level timing traces
OperationsWho owns failure, updates and rollback?Named owner and rehearsal

The worked example: a RAG assistant in a regulated VPC

Consider a fictional financial-services customer asking for an internal research assistant. “Keep the data in our VPC” is an incomplete requirement. Clarify whether it prohibits public-internet transit, provider-managed processing, cross-region storage, or all of these. Record the answer instead of silently replacing a strict rule with one your preferred architecture can satisfy.

For this exercise, the agreed contract is:

  • Prompts, retrieved document text and generated answers may be processed only by customer-controlled compute in the specified account and region. The customer separately approves the document-storage service and its access path.
  • Runtime calls to provider-managed model services are excluded. Software and model updates use an approved import process.
  • Each answer may use only documents the current reader is authorized to access. Authentication through SSO establishes identity; source permissions still decide access.
  • The target is a complete answer within two seconds under a workload to be measured. Streaming is permitted but does not replace the completion target.

A contract or private route cannot be assumed to override these requirements. AWS documents that Bedrock interface endpoints provide private connectivity without an internet gateway or NAT. That is a statement about the connection to the service, not proof that provider-managed inference satisfies this exercise's customer-controlled-compute rule. The distinction was checked against AWS documentation in November 2026. If the customer permits that processing later, record the new boundary and assess the service on its actual terms.

The architecture

Choose a self-hosted model for this contract, with permission-aware retrieval and evaluation inside the approved environment. The diagram shows the request path; ingestion must also preserve source identity, document version and access metadata.

rendering diagram…

Do not send disallowed documents to the model and then hope an answer filter removes them. Bind citations to the source version actually used, and apply the same permission checks when opening a citation. Cached answers, summaries and conversation history can contain protected facts too. Define their reuse and invalidation policy when a user's access changes. The RAG design bank provides deeper exercises on retrieval and evaluation.

Audit records should support diagnosis without becoming an uncontrolled copy of every prompt and response. Decide which identifiers, policy outcomes and timings are necessary, who can inspect payload samples, and how retention and deletion work. “Log everything inside the VPC” is not a complete data-handling design.

Spending the latency budget

Use one explicit trace before discussing percentiles. Assume the following sequential times for a fictional request:

StageTime
Identity and permission resolution50 ms
Retrieval180 ms
Reranking120 ms
Model admission queue150 ms
Prefill through first output token400 ms

First content arrives after 900 ms. Suppose subsequent output is delivered at a constant 40 tokens/s, with no buffering, and the answer has 120 tokens including the first. Completion is 0.900 + (120 - 1) / 40 = 3.875 seconds. The design misses the two-second complete-answer target even though the stream starts promptly.

Under exactly those assumptions, at most 45 output tokens fit: 0.900 + (45 - 1) / 40 = 2.000 seconds. That is a calculation, not a recommendation to truncate useful answers. Decide whether a short cited response still completes the task, whether model throughput can improve at the required quality, or whether the customer should change the completion contract. Removing the 120 ms reranker alone does not close a 1.875-second gap.

These are deterministic component inputs, not component p99s that can be added to claim an end-to-end p99. Measure whole requests at the agreed arrival process, prompt/output mix and cache state. Retain timeouts and failures. Give retries the same end-to-end budget rather than a fresh timeout on every attempt; the Premium retry-policy exercise works through that boundary.

The model decision

Self-hosting fits the chosen processing rule; it does not guarantee adequate quality, throughput or operational readiness. Size and benchmark the model, provision its dependencies, and name the owner for capacity, updates and rollback. A hosted service becomes a candidate only if the customer changes or clarifies the permitted processing boundary to include it. Then compare the actual task results and operating cost. Neither “hosted means no operations” nor “self-hosted means compliance is solved” is a defensible design claim.

The evaluation plan is not optional

Agree on representative tasks, evidence labels and a release decision with the customer. Score source retrieval, answer correctness, support for claims and abstention separately. Test permission changes and denied-source cases at the model-input boundary as well as the final answer. Zero observed unauthorized exposures is required for the selected test cases; it does not prove zero risk across every possible request.

Run the selected cases when the model, retrieval configuration, permission mapping or prompt changes. Include failure behavior: identity lookup unavailable, no permitted evidence, slow model and broken citations. Define when the product abstains, queues or returns an error, and verify that fallback paths preserve the same access boundary. The system-design bank and ML infrastructure questions extend the operational side. Evaluation evidence informs the customer's acceptance review; it cannot promise a regulatory or security approval.

How to practice this round

Write a one-page decision record with the processing boundary, latency definition, permission rule, chosen deployment and unresolved measurements. Rehearse a change: the customer now allows a named managed model service, or requires 120-token answers within two seconds. Revise only the decisions that follow from that change and identify what must be measured again. Use the must-know questions to test the prerequisites you could not explain without notes.

The one-line version

Defend each component with a customer requirement and each performance claim with a measurement contract. Leave the review with a testable design, a named owner for its unresolved risks, and a clear condition that would change your recommendation.
