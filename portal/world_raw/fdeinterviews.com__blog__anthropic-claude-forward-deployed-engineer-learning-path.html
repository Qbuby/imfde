Anthropic's Claude Forward Deployed Engineer Path: What the 11 Courses Cover, and What FDE Interviews Still Test

Anthropic's Claude FDE learning path on Skilljar is 11 courses and about 9.5 hours, ending in a pilot plan. What each course teaches, who can take it, how it relates to Frontier Academy and the certifications, and the interview rounds it does not prepare you for.

BY EMILY CARTER · FDEINTERVIEWS EDITORIAL · UPDATED NOVEMBER 19, 2026 · 9 MIN READ

Anthropic's Claude Forward Deployed Engineer path is a self-paced set of 11 courses on its partner Skilljar site, about 9.5 hours in all, whose stated goal is to "take a customer from a first use case to a working pilot, on site." Four core courses cover what Claude can and cannot do and how to choose a model. Five building courses cover the Claude platform, Claude Code, MCP and agent skills. Two delivery courses cover pilot design and enterprise adoption. You finish able to write a pilot plan: the baseline, the success measures agreed before building, the first slice to ship and the first wave of users. Our read after mapping every course to the FDE interview loop: it is a strong platform and pilot primer, and it leaves the timed case round, evaluation depth and the security conversation to you.

The path went around LinkedIn this week as "Anthropic has introduced a Claude Forward Deployed Engineer learning path." Everything below was checked on Anthropic's own pages on 19 November 2026. Course lists and durations change, so treat the numbers as dated.

What is in the path

The path page groups the courses into three blocks and gives a duration for each. The figure shows them in order, with the bar length set by the minutes.

The arithmetic is ours, from the durations on the page, and the shares do not add to exactly 100 because of rounding. The split says something about how Anthropic sees the job. More than half the time goes to building with Claude, and the customer-facing delivery work gets about a hundred minutes. The page also says the path shares nine courses with the Claude Developer path, so someone who finished that path has two courses and about 100 minutes left, the same total as the two delivery courses.

What each block teaches, in the page's own framing:

  • Core. "AI Capabilities and Limitations" is about "the limits you'll be explaining to users from the first day of a pilot." "AI Fluency for Builders" applies Anthropic's 4D framework (delegation, description, discernment and diligence) to building. "Claude Platform and Model Foundations" covers "the four decisions you make before writing a prompt: entry point, capability features, model, and context." A ten-minute module on Claude Academy covers which Claude model fits which task and how each draws on your rate limit.
  • Building. "Claude Platform 101" runs from the first API call through the agent loop, tools, extended thinking, skills, MCP, context management and managed agents. "Claude Code 101" is framed as "the tool you prototype with in the customer's repository," and "Claude Code in action" covers long, hands-off sessions controlled with permission modes, hooks and verification. The MCP course connects Claude to the customer's systems, and the agent skills course packages "what works so the customer's team can reuse it."
  • Delivering the pilot. "Delivery Methodology" covers qualifying the use case, designing the pilot, capturing the baseline and managing adoption and risk. "Driving enterprise adoption of Claude" follows a customer "from activation to scale," including enablement, launch and finding the next workload.

Who can take it

The registration panel on the path page says the path is "Open to Claude Partner Network members," and the stated prerequisite is that you build software and have worked directly with client teams.

If you are not at a partner firm, much of the material is still reachable. On 19 November 2026, Anthropic's public Academy catalog listed courses with the same titles as seven of the eleven: AI Capabilities and Limitations, AI Fluency for Builders, Claude Platform 101, Claude Code 101, Claude Code in Action, Introduction to Model Context Protocol and Introduction to agent skills. Claude Platform and Model Foundations and both delivery courses did not appear there, and the model-choice module lives on Claude Academy. A matching title is not proof of identical content, so check before you assume a public course counts toward anything.

Path, Academy and certifications: which is which

Three different things are being discussed under one name, and in an interview it helps to know which one someone means.

Frontier Academy was announced on 2 October 2026 with a $100 million commitment and a goal of 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers by the end of 2027. Its first cohorts, in San Francisco, New York and London, include engineers from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk. Engineers work through a simulated enterprise deployment "from the right use case through security review to handover," take a graded practical, and then lead a real Claude use case at their own organization for 12 weeks. Anthropic expects the first Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer badges in early 2027. The online path is the open front door to the same body of knowledge, without the assessment.

After the path, the page suggests three directions: the Developer Professional exam if your engagements move toward production (agent architecture, reliability, observability, evaluation, cost and latency, and security), Architect Professional prep if you lead solution design, and a Claude Code partner badge of eight courses, about 12 hours, a capstone and an assessment if you deploy Claude Code for customers.

The deliverable: a pilot plan in the shape the path asks for

The path's stated outcome is a document, and it is worth seeing one. Here is a pilot plan for a hypothetical use case, written to the four parts the page names. The numbers are invented for the example.

Use case. An insurer's claims intake team reads emailed first notices of loss and keys them into the claims system. Claude drafts the structured record; an adjuster approves or corrects it.

Baseline, measured before building. Two weeks of the current process on 400 notices: median 11 minutes of handling per notice, 6% of records later corrected by a second adjuster, 2.1 days from receipt to a claim number.

Success measures, agreed before building. Median handling time under 5 minutes on the same mix of notices. Correction rate no worse than 6%. No record created without an adjuster's approval. Measured weekly on a sample the customer's team labels, not on the vendor's test set.

First slice. Auto and home notices that arrive as a single email with no attachments, about 40% of volume. Notices with attachments, multiple claimants or a litigation flag stay manual in the pilot.

First wave. Six adjusters on one shift, chosen by the team lead, with a daily fifteen-minute review of every correction for the first two weeks. Expansion to the next shift only when two consecutive weeks meet the measures.

Every line of that plan is a sentence an interviewer can push on. Why 400 notices? Who labels the weekly sample, and how do you stop them grading generously? What happens to the 60% you left out? Practicing the plan is good. Practicing the defense of each line is what turns it into interview performance. Our pilot success metrics question and the pilot acceptance criteria concept work through exactly that.

What the path covers, set against the interview loop

FDE interviews at Anthropic and elsewhere usually include a build or use-case screen, a solution design round, a case or decomposition round, a customer conversation and a coding round. The Anthropic FDE interview guide has the details. This is our reading of how far the path takes you in each.

The largest gap is evaluation. The path teaches you to capture a baseline and agree measures, but how to build an evaluation set from real failures, check that an LLM judge agrees with people, and gate a release on repeated runs sits in the Professional exam prep, not in this path. Our eval loop concept is the shortest route through it.

The second gap is time pressure. A course lets you think for as long as you like. A case round gives you a vague business problem and thirty minutes. The decomposition round guide and the customer simulation guide are built for that.

How we would use it in a six-week plan

If you are preparing for FDE interviews and can access the path:

  1. Week 1. Take the four core courses. Write one page, in your own words, on what Claude should not be trusted to do in your target industry. You will use it in every customer conversation.
  2. Weeks 2 and 3. Take the five building courses with a real repository open. Build one small MCP server against a fake internal system, and one skill that packages a repeatable task. These become the projects you talk about.
  3. Week 4. Take the two delivery courses, then write three pilot plans like the one above for use cases you pick at random from our behavioral and customer questions. Time yourself.
  4. Weeks 5 and 6. Spend the time the path does not cover: evaluation, a security review conversation and timed case rounds. Our six-week plan lays out the rest.

If you cannot access the path, the seven public courses cover most of weeks 1 to 3, and the delivery material is what this site's pilot and adoption questions are for, including the one about adoption stuck at 12% after ninety days.

What to say about it in an interview

If an interviewer asks how you prepared, name what you built rather than what you completed. "I took the Claude FDE path" is a line. "I wrote a pilot plan for a claims intake use case, with a baseline I could measure in two weeks and a first slice that kept attachments out of scope, and here is what I would cut if the security review came back late" is an answer. The path gives you the vocabulary. The interview checks whether you can use it under pressure, on a problem you have not seen.
